The day his wings were taken, the sky seemed farther away than ever.

He was still a child when he learned that the blood running through his veins could be both a gift and a curse. Through his mother, he carried the heritage of the Skyrith. Through his father, he bore the blood of humankind. Yet neither world had a place for someone born of both.

To the Skyrith, his existence was an offense against the order they sought to preserve. His mixed blood made him an outsider, and his imperfect wings became a mark of shame that he could never conceal.

Then came the day they took them from him.

He remembered little of the pain itself. What remained in his memory was the feeling of something being torn away — not merely from his body, but from the very essence of who he was meant to be. The wings that should have carried him among the clouds were gone, leaving only scars where his birthright once rested.

His mother could do nothing to spare him.

She had already been condemned. Her fate was sealed, and no plea could turn the judgment aside. Whatever reasons lay behind her sentence, the world had shown no mercy to a mother whose only crime was giving birth to a child who did not belong.

He would never forget her final moments.

Nor would he forget the silence that followed.

With his mother gone and his father nothing more than a name he had never known, the child was left alone in a world that had already rejected him. There was no home to return to, no family to call upon, and no wings to carry him away.

The sky remained above him, vast and unreachable.

And somewhere beyond the lands of the Skyrith, a small child began a life he had never chosen — a life as an outcast, with nothing but the will to survive.
