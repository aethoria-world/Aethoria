## The Severance

The boy had never seen the great hall so crowded.

Skyrith stood in rows beneath its towering pillars, their wings folded behind them. Some watched him with cold indifference; others whispered among themselves, their voices fading whenever he turned towards them.

He searched the gathering faces for his mother.

She was nowhere to be seen.

A hand rested upon his shoulder, guiding him forward. He hesitated, but the grip tightened.

"Keep walking."

He obeyed.

At the far end of the hall, several figures sat above the others. Their expressions were difficult to read, but none looked upon him with kindness.

"Bring the child closer," one of them commanded.

The boy took another step.

"Look at him," another voice said. "A child of two bloodlines, standing before us as though he belongs here."

"He does not belong here."

A murmur passed through the hall.

The boy lowered his gaze. He did not understand why so many people had gathered, nor why they looked at him as though he had done something terrible. He only knew that he wanted his mother.

"His existence is an offence against our blood," said one of the elders. "We have dealt with such abominations before. None were permitted to live."

"Then why does this one still breathe?" another asked.

Silence followed.

For a moment, no one answered.

"Because we have something to learn from him."

The boy looked up.

The speaker continued.

"Others of mixed blood were put to death. This one shall be given a different fate. We will take away the wings he inherited from his mother's people and leave him beyond our borders."

"And if he survives?"

"Then we shall know that even without the gift of the Skyrith, he can endure."

"And if he does not?"

"Then his fate will be no different from the others."

The words meant little to the boy. He could not understand why they spoke of his life as though it were something to be measured, or why his mother had not come to stand beside him.

He turned towards the entrance.

"Where is my mother?"

No one answered.

***

They took him away before he could ask again.

The chamber where they brought him was unfamiliar. Its stone walls offered no comfort, and the figures surrounding him remained silent.

He struggled when they held him in place.

"Mother!" he cried.

His voice echoed through the chamber.

No answer came.

He had always admired the wings of the Skyrith. They were more than a means of flight; they were a part of who they were. As a child, he had imagined his own wings carrying him high above the mountains, where the clouds drifted like endless rivers across the sky.

He had never imagined a life without them.

Then the pain came.

He cried out, twisting against the hands that restrained him. He could not understand what they were doing, only that something precious was being torn away from him.

He called for his mother again.

But she did not come.

When it was over, the boy could barely move. His body trembled, and every breath seemed to remind him of what was gone.

He turned his head, searching behind him.

There was nothing.

His wings were gone.

***

The boy was carried beyond the borders of the Skyrith lands.

No one told him where they were taking him. No one explained why his mother had not come. He was too weak to ask, and the journey passed in a blur of pain and exhaustion.

At last, they stopped.

The ground beneath him was rough. The air was cold, and unfamiliar mountains stretched across the distant horizon.

One of the Skyrith lowered him to the ground.

The boy tried to hold on.

"Please..."

The figure pulled away.

For a brief moment, the boy looked up at the wings folded behind the stranger's back. He watched as they opened, catching the wind with effortless grace.

Then the Skyrith rose into the air.

The others followed.

The boy watched them disappear into the distance.

No one looked back.

***

Night approached.

He tried to stand, but his legs could not bear his weight. With trembling hands, he pushed himself against the earth, only to collapse again.

Above him, the sky stretched endlessly.

He remembered watching the Skyrith fly through it. He remembered believing that one day he would join them.

Now the sky seemed farther away than ever.

He did not know where he was. He did not know whether his mother was alive. He did not even know why he had been spared when the others had not.

All he knew was that he had been left alone.

The wind grew colder as darkness settled over the land. The boy curled against the ground, trying to keep himself warm.

He had no shelter. No food. No one to call for help.

The Skyrith had taken his wings and abandoned him beyond their borders, believing that the world would finish what they had begun.

They had left him to die.

But the world had other plans.
